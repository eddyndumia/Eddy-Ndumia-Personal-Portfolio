## 3am and the Terminal

There's a version of me that only exists at 3am. Everyone else is asleep, the house is quiet in a way it never is during the day, and it's just me, a cursor blinking, and whatever bug decided tonight was the night it would finally show itself. I used to think this was just bad time management. Now I think it's closer to the only hour where nothing is asking anything of me except the problem in front of me.

There's something almost meditative about debugging at that hour. No Slack. No calls. No one checking in. Just cause and effect, laid out in logs, waiting for you to be patient enough to read them properly instead of guessing. It's one of the few places left where effort and result still track each other honestly. You put in the careful hour, you find the off-by-one error. You skip the careful hour, it finds you tomorrow instead, usually worse.

I won't pretend the hour is romantic. It's mostly just tired. But there's a kind of honesty in tired work that rested work doesn't have — you can't perform confidence you don't feel, you can't fake understanding a stack trace, either you see it or you don't. Maybe that's why I keep ending up back there, at the terminal, past the hour I told myself I'd stop. It's not that I don't value sleep. It's that the terminal doesn't ask me to be anything other than exactly as sharp, or as tired, as I actually am.

Some people find their stillness on a run, or in prayer, or staring at water. Mine, apparently, is a black screen and green text at an hour polite people are asleep. I'm not sure that's healthy. I'm fairly sure it's honest. For now, honest is the version of myself I trust the most.
