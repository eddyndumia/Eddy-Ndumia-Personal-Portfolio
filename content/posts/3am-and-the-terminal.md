---
title: "3am and the Terminal"
date: 2026-09-18
---

There's a version of me that only shows up at 3am. Everyone's asleep, the house is actually quiet for once, and it's just me and whatever bug decided tonight was the night it would finally show up. The only light is the screen. The only sound is the fan on the laptop working harder than it should.

I used to file this under bad time management. Everyone says sleep is where the real work gets done, and they're not wrong. But the older I get the more I think 3am is just the one hour left in the day where nothing's asking anything of me except the problem in front of me.

Think about what a normal day is like when you're building something on your own. Messages from clients. Someone asking if you've seen their email. The phone buzzing with M-Pesa alerts, group chats, notifications from five apps. Family stuff. Every hour is chopped into pieces by something that needs you for two minutes. You can get a lot done in a day like that, but you almost never get deep. You're always half somewhere else.

At 3am all of that is gone.

Debugging at that hour is close to meditative if I'm honest. No Slack, no calls, nobody checking in. Just logs, waiting for me to actually read them instead of guessing and hoping. Put in the careful hour and you find the off-by-one. Skip it because you're tired and it's late, and it finds you tomorrow instead, usually worse, usually in front of someone.

Here's what that hour actually looks like for me.

I stop guessing. During the day I'll change something, rerun it, change something else, hoping to get lucky. At 3am I don't have the energy for that, so I do the boring thing. Read the error. Read the log. Follow what the code is actually doing, line by line.

I write things down. A plain text file with what I've tried and what happened. Half the time the bug shows up while I'm writing the note, not while I'm staring at the code.

I know when to stop. If I've fixed it, I commit and close the laptop. If I haven't, I write down exactly where I got to so tomorrow's me doesn't start from zero.

I won't call it romantic, it's mostly just tired. But there's an honesty in tired work that rested work doesn't really have. You can't fake understanding a stack trace to yourself at 3am. Either you see it or you don't. Nobody is watching, so there's no point pretending. Maybe that's the real reason I keep ending up back here, past the hour I told myself I'd stop.

I'm not going to pretend it's all good. I've written before about how the 100-hour week thing is a lie, and I meant it. Some 3am sessions are just me being stubborn when I should be asleep, and I pay for it the next day with a slow head and short patience. There's a difference between choosing the quiet because the problem needs it and ending up there every night because the day got away from you. I don't always get that right.

Some people find their quiet on a run, or praying, or staring at water till their head empties out. Mine's a black terminal at an hour normal people are supposed to be asleep. Not sure that's healthy. Pretty sure it's honest.

Right now, honest is the version of myself I trust the most.
