---
title: "3am and the Terminal"
date: 2026-09-18
---

There's a version of me that only exists at 3am. Everyone else is asleep, the house has gone quiet in a way it never is during daylight hours, and it's just me, a blinking cursor, and whatever bug decided tonight was finally the night it would show its face after hiding all week. I used to file this under "bad time management" and leave it there. I don't think that's the whole story anymore. I think it's closer to: this is the only hour left where nothing is asking anything of me except the one problem directly in front of me.

There's something almost meditative about debugging at that hour, if I'm being honest about it, which I'm trying to be more of lately. No Slack pinging. No calls to join. Nobody checking in on progress. Just cause and effect, laid out in logs, patiently waiting for me to be patient enough to actually read them instead of guessing and hoping. It's one of maybe three places left in my life where effort and result still track each other honestly. Put in the careful hour, find the off-by-one error. Skip the careful hour because you're tired and it's late and surely it's fine — it finds *you* instead, tomorrow, usually worse, usually in front of someone.

## not romantic, just honest

I won't dress the hour up as romantic, because it isn't. It's mostly just tired. But there's an honesty in tired work that rested work doesn't quite have the same version of — you can't perform a confidence you don't feel at 3am, you can't fake understanding a stack trace to yourself when there's no one else in the room to fake it *for*. Either you see it or you don't. And maybe that's the actual reason I keep ending up back here, past the hour I promised myself I'd stop. It's not that I don't value sleep — I do, increasingly, as a scarce resource I keep spending badly. It's that the terminal has never once asked me to be anything other than exactly as sharp, or exactly as tired, as I actually am that night. No performance required. Just the log, and me, and whatever's actually true.

Some people find their version of stillness on a run, or in prayer, or staring at water until their head goes quiet. Mine, it turns out, is a black terminal window and green text at an hour polite people are supposed to be asleep. I'm not entirely convinced that's healthy. I'm fairly convinced it's honest. And right now, at this particular stage of building things with no guarantees attached, honest is the version of myself I trust the most — even if he does keep me up way later than he should.
