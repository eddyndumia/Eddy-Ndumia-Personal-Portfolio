---
title: "Pay for the Job Abroad Only When the Job Is Real"
date: 2026-10-03T12:00:00+03:00
---

In July a Kenyan job seeker paid an agency KES 110,000 for a job in Qatar. There was no job. The DCI arrested the agency's director, which is more than happens in most of these cases, but the money was already gone.

If you're Kenyan you've heard this story before, probably more than once. Someone's cousin paid for a job in Saudi. Someone's neighbour sold a cow to pay "processing fees" for Canada. Someone sat in an office on a Monday with their passport and their certificates, paid, got a receipt with a stamp on it, and then the phone numbers stopped going through. Sometimes the agency is fake from the start. Sometimes it's licensed and still takes the money and stalls for months. Either way the person who paid has nothing to hold on to except a receipt and a WhatsApp chat.

This keeps happening because of how the money moves. The job seeker pays first, in full, before anything is real. The agency holds all the money and all the information. If nothing happens, the only way to get anything back is to chase them, go to the police, or wait for the government to do something.

The government is trying. The Senate has grilled the National Employment Authority over rogue agencies that stay on the list. The Labour ministry has been telling agencies to refund people or face legal action. NEA puts the approved list on its website and tells everyone to check it. All of that helps a bit. But refunds after the fact almost never happen, and a list on a website doesn't stop a licensed agency from taking your money and doing nothing.

And the number of people paying these fees is going up. Kenya is actively pushing more people to work abroad, there are new deals with Canada, Italy, other countries in Europe. More people going means more fees, and more fees means more places for the money to disappear.

So the gap isn't information. Everyone already knows to be careful. The gap is that there's nothing between your money and the agency. Once you pay, it's theirs.

I kept thinking about this while looking for a crypto idea worth building. Most of what gets built in crypto right now is trading, payments infrastructure, or AI agent stuff. Very little of it touches a problem a normal Kenyan family actually has. This one does, and it happens to be a problem that a smart contract fits really well. A contract can hold money that nobody, not the agency, not the job seeker, not even us, can take out except by rules everyone agreed on upfront.

So that's what we're building. It's called KaziSafe.

Kazi is work in Swahili. The whole thing is about making sure the money you pay for work abroad is safe until the work is real.

Here's how it works, as simple as I can put it.

You're a job seeker and you've found an agency. Instead of paying the agency, you pay into KaziSafe, by M-Pesa like you'd pay anything else. The money gets locked in escrow on the blockchain. The agency can see it's there, so they know you're serious, but they can't touch it yet.

The agency gets paid in stages, and only when each stage is proven. When there's a real job offer, and we can confirm it came from the employer's real email domain and wasn't edited, the agency gets a part. When the visa is issued, they get another part. When you start working and your first salary comes in, they get the rest. If the deadline passes and the job never happens, the money that hasn't been released goes back to your M-Pesa automatically. You don't have to chase anyone.

Every agency on KaziSafe also gets a public record. How many people they've sent abroad, how many refunds, how long it took. That record lives on the blockchain, so nobody can edit it or delete it, including the agency and including us.

A few things I care about getting right, because without them this is just another escrow website.

The job seeker never touches crypto. No wallet, no seed phrase, nothing to install. You pay by M-Pesa and if there's a refund it comes back to M-Pesa. The blockchain is there so the money is held by rules and not by a person, but you shouldn't have to know it exists.

Proof has to come from the source, not from the agency's word. An agency saying "the visa is out" isn't proof. The offer has to come from the employer's actual email, and the visa and the salary have to come from the actual systems that show them. That's the part that takes the most work and it's the part that makes it worth anything.

The refund has to be automatic. If getting your money back depends on someone at KaziSafe approving it, we've just built another agency. The deadline is in the contract, and after it passes anyone can trigger the refund.

Honest agencies have to win. Most agencies aren't scammers, and right now they look exactly the same as the ones that are. A record you can't fake is the best thing a good agency can have. If job seekers start asking "are you on KaziSafe?" before they pay, the fake ones lose business without anyone having to arrest them.

The agency pays the fee, not the job seeker. A small cut of each payment we release to the agency. Refunds are free.

What this changes, if it works, is pretty simple. Right now the risk sits completely on the person with the least money, the one trying to get a job. KaziSafe moves it to the agency, which is where it should have been all along. And there's nothing Kenya-specific about it. Uganda, the Philippines, Nepal, India, Bangladesh, every country that sends people abroad to work has the same problem. The Philippines already makes agencies lodge a deposit with the government so workers can claim from it later. That shows the idea works, it's just slow and it doesn't protect each person's actual fee.

I'm not going to pretend this is easy.

Getting proof out of visa portals and salary systems is real work, and every country does it differently. For the first version the offer proof is real and the visa and salary proofs are demo ones, until we build them properly for one route, probably Kenya to Saudi. Agencies might not want to use it, especially the ones who were planning to keep the money. Some costs are legitimately upfront, like medicals, and those need a clean way to be paid without breaking the whole idea. Moving shillings in and out of stablecoins has to be cheap and reliable. And we're doing this on basically no budget.

So the plan is small. We're entering KaziSafe into a Solana hackathon that closes on October 12, with a working version where an agency creates a placement, a job seeker pays by M-Pesa, the offer gets proven, and the money either gets released or comes back. While building it I'm going to talk to people who've paid agencies, the ones who got jobs and the ones who got scammed, and to a few licensed agencies. If the agencies say no to all of it, we learn why. If a few say yes, we go live with them on one route and do it properly.

If you've paid an agency for a job abroad, whatever happened, I'd really like to hear how it went. That's the most useful thing anyone can give me right now.
