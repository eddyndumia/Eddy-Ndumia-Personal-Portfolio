I made a game. It's called Aura Farm and it's probably the most brainrot thing I've ever built.

Slang falls from the top of the screen. You catch the Ws, RIZZ, HIM, and you dodge the Ls, MID, CRINGE, NPC. A RATIO takes two of your hearts. Catch enough in a row and the multiplier kicks in, and the more aura you farm in one run the more your character evolves. You start as Lirilì Larilà and if you're actually him you end the run as Tung Tung Tung Sahur, with the whole screen going gold.

If you've been anywhere near a kid or TikTok this year you know these characters. Italian brainrot. AI-made animals and objects with names that sound Italian, a shark in Nike sneakers, a coffee cup that's a ballerina, a wooden log with a bat. Kids know every one of them by name. That's half of why I built it. The other half is money. I wanted to see what a small ad-supported game actually makes, and the only honest way to find out is to put one out and watch.

The characters were the first real problem. I wanted Pepe and Gigachad in there too and they're not. Pepe's artist goes after people, and Gigachad is photos of a real person. One takedown on Google Play and the ad account goes with it, and that's the same account other apps make money on. The Italian brainrot images on Wikimedia Commons are public domain, an AI made them and nobody owns them, so those are the ones in the game, cut out of their backgrounds.

The second thing I didn't plan for was kids. A game like this gets played by kids whether you want it or not, and Google has rules for that. So the first time you open it, it asks the year you were born. Under 13 you only get safe, non-personalised ads. It makes less money. It's still the right call.

It's TypeScript on a plain canvas, no game engine, so the same code runs in the browser and as the Android app. Nothing leaves your phone, there's no account and nothing to sign up for. I spent a while making it fast on cheap phones because that's what most people here have.

Right now you can play it in the browser or download the Android app from GitHub. Google Play wants fourteen days of testing with at least twelve testers before a new app can go public, so the Play Store version comes around the middle of October. If you want to be one of the testers, send me your Gmail.
